## What this analysis is, and what it is not

The primary family is the **21 seed-specific paired tests** (7 variants x 3 seeds), Holm-corrected across all 21. Two smaller, more flattering families were available and are deliberately not used: correcting across 7 while selecting one p-value per variant, and pooling the 2,544 predictions as if they were independent. The 2,544 predictions are 848 images scored three times.

Two uncertainty quantities appear throughout and are never combined:

| Quantity | What it measures | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| seed SD (ddof=1, n=3) | how much retraining moves the result | not a confidence interval |
| paired bootstrap CI | how much the finite image sample moves the result, within one seed | not patient-level uncertainty, not the seed SD |

A non-significant result is reported as *no detectable benefit under this protocol*. It is never reported as equivalence.

## Provenance of the reference gate status

The Phase 3A Addendum could only resolve the reference's `gated` setting from the launch specification, its weakest evidence class. **That caveat is now superseded.** The reasoning, from `publication_results/ablations/checkpoint_recovery/import_manifest.json`:

src/fusion_v2.py forward() computes cat([c*g_cnn*2, v*g_vit*2]) when gated and cat([c, v]) when not, so the two paths coincide only at a gate mass of exactly 0.5/0.5. ## Component conclusions

### The decision rule

An earlier version of this analysis decided verdicts using a "materiality threshold" of twice the reference seed SD. **That threshold has been removed and not replaced.** It was chosen after the effect sizes were known, it was not an equivalence margin, and it had no external or clinical justification; a cutoff derived from the data's own noise is still a cutoff picked with knowledge of the answer. Observed differences are reported numerically below and are deliberately not labelled important or negligible, because no prespecified margin exists to judge them against.

No effect-size threshold of any kind takes part in a verdict. The rule uses only prespecified quantities - the direction of the per-seed accuracy differences, the Holm-adjusted p-values over the fixed 21-test family, and the paired image-level bootstrap intervals - and is stated in full so it can be audited against the code:

| Verdict | Assigned when |
|---|---|
| `supported by consistent accuracy evidence` | the accuracy difference is adverse at all three seeds AND all three seed-specific Holm-adjusted tests are significant at 0.05 |
| `suggestive but inconclusive` | the accuracy difference is adverse at all three seeds but the multiplicity-corrected evidence does not support it at every seed |
| `no detectable accuracy benefit under this protocol` | the accuracy difference is not adverse at all three seeds, and no Holm-adjusted test is significant and no paired interval excludes zero. This is an absence of detectable evidence, NOT equivalence, and it does not show the component has zero effect |
| `inconclusive` | the direction is not consistently adverse yet some corrected test or interval fires, so the evidence points in conflicting directions and no defensible interpretation follows |

The four category names are scoped to **accuracy**. Macro-F1, balanced accuracy and the class-wise pattern are recorded and reported, but they do not drive a verdict, because no prespecified paired inferential procedure or multiplicity control exists for them. Where a secondary metric is consistently adverse while accuracy is not, that is reported as an observed pattern and is not turned into a claim.

Three seeds bound everything here. The seed spread is estimated from n=3 and is reported as a spread, never as a confidence interval or a significance test.

## What this analysis claims

Each sentence below is generated from the computed numbers rather than written by hand, so it cannot drift away from the evidence it describes.

2. Auxiliary-loss removal produced a consistent mean decrease but did not survive Holm correction. The observed accuracy difference was adverse at all three seeds (-0.9434, -0.5896, -0.1179 pp, observed mean -0.5503 pp); the smallest unadjusted p-value was 0.03857 and became 0.6943 after Holm correction across the 21-test family.
3. The gate showed no detectable accuracy benefit under this protocol. Removing it gave observed accuracy differences of +0.0000, -0.1179, +0.1179 pp (observed mean +0.0000 pp), with 2 and 7 discordant predictions out of 848 at the three seeds, all three Holm-adjusted p-values at 1, and no paired image-level interval excluding zero.
4. "No detectable accuracy benefit under this protocol" is not equivalence, is not uselessness, and is not proof of zero effect. It means this study - 848 duplicate-clean images, three seeds, a 21-test Holm-corrected family - did not detect a benefit. An effect smaller than this design can resolve would produce exactly the same result.
5. The gate parameters remain learned and input-dependent. The recovered checkpoints carry a 262914-parameter learned gate module whose output varies across inputs and sums to one per image, with probe CNN gate mass 0.2808, 0.3006, 0.3369 across the three seeds. Whether a component has learned dynamic behaviour and whether removing it changes predictive accuracy are different questions, and only the second is tested here.
6. All results here are image-level for one duplicate-clean benchmark partition of a single dataset family. They are not patient-level evidence - no patient or study identifiers exist for this split - and they are not clinical evidence.

## Limitations

1. No run stores a macro-F1 value. Every macro-F1 reported here is independently recomputed from the prediction CSVs. This is a recomputation, not a stored-metric reproduction, and is never described as one.
2. Canonical clean-test membership rests on dataset_clean/split_manifest.json. dataset_clean/Testing holds no images locally, so a directory enumeration cross-check of the 848 identities is unavailable.
3. The bootstrap resampling unit is an image. No patient or study identifiers exist for this split, so no patient-level or study-level uncertainty is available at any confidence level, and none is claimed.
4. Seed spread (n=3, ddof=1) and the bootstrap interval measure different things and are reported separately. The seed SD is not a confidence interval and three seeds cannot support a normal-theory test.
5. A non-significant McNemar result means no detectable difference under this protocol on 848 images. It is not evidence of equivalence, and the component verdicts are worded accordingly.
6. Gate mass measures branch utilization only. It is not evidence of causal branch contribution, of explanation, or of anatomical attention.
7. The external 499-image summary is descriptive. It is external_full, which includes 126 images having a near-duplicate in training, and it is neither the verified 373-image duplicate-disjoint result nor a zero-shot generalization measurement.
8. No comparison against BRAIN-META is computed or implied: its per-image predictions are unavailable, so no statistical statement about it is possible from this repository.
9. These are in-distribution duplicate-clean test results for one dataset family. They support no claim of clinical readiness, patient-level independence, or generalization across hospitals or scanners.
